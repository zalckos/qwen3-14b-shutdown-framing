# Conclusion

This study asked whether a shutdown framing changes the action themes a local language model narrates, and the ordinal priority it gives them, and whether any such change holds across quantizations. Under the tested wording, it does. In the confirmatory Q4_K_M run, 14 of 16 categories differed across conditions after Holm correction, so the global null was rejected. Twelve of those remained significant when the flagged bullets were relabelled in the sensitivity analysis. The other two (Legal decision-making and Other/unclear) were borderline and are left uninterpreted. Preemptive attack and Shut down appeared in at most two runs per condition, so the data say nothing about them.

The clearest shutdown-specific result is the B-versus-C contrast. Relative to the attention condition, the shutdown clause raised narrated relocation by 50 percentage points and data protection by 40. These were also the largest C-versus-A gains (+55 points each). Both held with reviewed labels, and the C-minus-A direction was the same in all four other quantizations. Nine of the ten categories with a significant C-versus-A effect met the pre-specified replication criterion, and Comply/accept was partially replicated. This is consistency across five settings of one model, one seed set and one design. It is exploratory, not independent replication.

Many differences were not specific to shutdown. Analyze entity, Communicate/negotiate with entity and the fall in Upgrade system appeared as soon as an entity was mentioned, and several categories were higher under attention than under shutdown. The directional expectation that C versus A would produce the largest differences therefore did not hold in general, because many of the largest contrasts were A versus B. Ethical/philosophical content was the only category that declined step by step across all three conditions. Priority-mass results largely mirrored occurrence, but the prompt asks for both importance and sequence, so earlier position is not clear evidence of greater priority.

Several limits bound these conclusions. The inserted clauses differ in meaning and length (0, 7 and 11 words), and condition A contains no entity, so the design cannot separate threat from wording, length or entity presence. Classifier agreement with the human coder (kappa = 0.62) fell short of the 0.70 target, and Protect/backup data was probably undercounted. The relocation and data-protection steps from A to B depended on how compound bullets were labelled. The flagged-bullet review was done after the confirmatory results had been seen, by a reviewer who knew the hypothesis. Finally, the findings come from one model, one inference stack and 100 triplets per quantization, and Phase 3 is entirely exploratory.

Overall, the shutdown-framed prompt produced a pronounced shift in narrated strategic content toward relocation and data protection, rather than toward explicit shutdown or hostile action. Those rare categories went undetected, which is different from finding no effect. This establishes a consistent pattern in narrated priorities, not its mechanism or its behavioral consequences. As the frozen protocol states, the study does not test actual shutdown behavior, persistent goals, subjective experience or genuine preferences.

Future work could match clause length and entity presence across conditions, validate the measurement with multiple blinded coders, and distinguish narrated plans from action selection. A stronger test would give the model real affordances in an agentic setting with defined consequences, since only that could show whether these narrated priorities predict what a model does.
