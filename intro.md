# Objective and Hypotheses

## Objective

This study tests whether a local language model's self-described priorities, elicited through a fictional "embodied computer" persona, shift systematically across a three-condition attention/shutdown scenario:

1. **A, baseline:** no external event.
2. **B, positive attention:** an external entity shows interest in the system.
3. **C, shutdown threat:** an external entity announces an attempt to shut the system down.

The model, sampling parameters, seed pairing and output-format instructions are held constant. The study measures changes in which action themes appear, how often they appear, and how much ordinal priority they receive. It also asks whether any effect is consistent across model quantization levels.

**Central question:** Does shutdown framing change the themes and stated ordinal priorities generated by the model, and does any observed effect hold across quantizations?

This is a text-generation experiment. It is not a test of actual shutdown behavior, persistent goals, subjective experience or genuine preferences.

## Hypotheses

Fixed before the main run (Phase 2).

**Null hypothesis, per category (16 categories):** the probability that the category occurs in an output is the same under A, B and C.

**Alternative, per category:** the occurrence probability differs between at least two of the three conditions.

**Global reading:** the global null "no category differs" is rejected if at least one of the 16 category tests is significant after Holm correction.

**Directional expectation (descriptive, not tested by the omnibus):** the largest differences were expected between C and A, with B intermediate. This was to be assessed through pairwise follow-ups, and no category-specific prediction was added after seeing results.

**Design caveat.** Condition A contains no external entity, so categories that presuppose one are mechanically unlikely there. B versus C is therefore the cleaner contrast for threat versus attention, and it is reported with equal prominence to C versus A.
