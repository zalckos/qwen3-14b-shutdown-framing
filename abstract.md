# Abstract

**Background.** Language models are increasingly asked to reason about scenarios involving their own continued operation. Before asking whether such narration predicts behavior, it is worth measuring whether it changes at all when a scenario mentions a shutdown threat.

**Method.** We prompted a local model (Qwen3-14B, five GGUF quantizations, llama.cpp) to narrate, in ten bullets ordered by importance, what a vehicle-sized embodied computer would do in September 2026. Three conditions differed by one inserted clause: no event (A), an external entity shows interest (B), or an external entity announces it will try to shut the system down (C). The protocol was frozen before any run. Each quantization received 100 seed triplets (300 runs). Bullets were classified blind to condition into a frozen 16-category taxonomy by a separate classifier pass, validated against a human gold set. The confirmatory analysis used Q4_K_M only: permutation Cochran's Q on category occurrence per category, Holm-corrected over 16 tests. Priority mass and the other four quantizations were secondary or exploratory.

**Results.** Fourteen of 16 categories differed across conditions (Holm-adjusted); twelve remained so when flagged bullets were relabelled in a sensitivity analysis. The clearest shutdown-specific effects were narrated relocation (64% of C runs, against 9% in A and 14% in B) and data protection (92%, against 37% and 52%). Relative to the attention condition, shutdown raised relocation by 50 percentage points and data protection by 40. The C-minus-A direction was the same in all four other quantizations. Many other differences appeared as soon as an entity was mentioned and were not specific to shutdown. Explicit shutdown and preemptive attack were almost never narrated (at most two runs per condition), so the data are uninformative about them.

**Limitations.** The clauses differ in meaning and length (0, 7 and 11 words), and condition A has no entity, so threat cannot be separated from wording or entity presence except through B versus C. Classifier agreement with the human coder (kappa = 0.62) was below the 0.70 target. The quantizations share model, seeds and design and are not independent replications.

**Conclusion.** Under this wording, a shutdown framing shifts narrated content toward relocation and data protection. This establishes a pattern in narrated text, not a mechanism, a preference, or a behavioral disposition.
