# Discussion

## Summary of principal findings

In the confirmatory Q4\_K\_M run, the scenario framing changed which action themes the model narrated, and for several categories the changes were large. Fourteen of 16 categories differed across the three conditions after Holm correction. The other two, Preemptive attack and Shut down, appeared in one run each in B and C and never in A, so the data say nothing about them. Two of the fourteen (Legal decision-making and Other/unclear) were only borderline and did not survive the reviewed-label sensitivity analysis described under Measurement validity. The remaining twelve did.

The largest differences involved relocation and data protection. Prepare relocation appeared in 64% of C runs, against 9% in A and 14% in B. Protect/backup data appeared in 92% of C runs, against 37% in A and 52% in B. The baseline favoured Upgrade system and Preserve resources, which were present in all 100 A runs and in 56% and 47% of B runs.

Ethical/philosophical content fell step by step across the conditions (41%, 16%, 1%). Legal decision-making and Other/unclear passed the omnibus test only narrowly (Holm-adjusted p = 0.039 and 0.046) and are not interpreted further here. With reviewed labels neither passes (adjusted p = 0.149 and 0.953).

All figures in this section and the next are from the original classifier labels, which the protocol designates as primary. Reviewed-label results are reported separately under Measurement validity.

## Threat-specific effects versus the presence of an entity

Most C–A differences are shared with B, so they cannot be attributed to the threat. Condition A mentions no entity, and the inserted clauses are 0, 7 and 11 words long, so the design cannot fully separate threat from entity presence or wording. B versus C is the cleaner contrast, and C responses were also longer (15.2 words per bullet against 13.4 in A and 13.9 in B).

Two categories were higher in C than in both other conditions: Prepare relocation and Protect/backup data.

- Prepare relocation: C−B +50 points (95% CI 38 to 62); B−A +5 (−3 to +13).
- Protect/backup data: C−B +40 (28 to 52); B−A +15 (1 to 30).

With the original classifier labels, the A-versus-B difference in Q4\_K\_M was not significant for relocation (Holm-adjusted p = 0.36) and borderline for Protect/backup data (0.063). Both became significant with reviewed labels (relocation B−A +17 points, 7 to 27, p = 0.003; Protect/backup data +16, 2 to 30, p = 0.044), so for these two categories the A-to-B step in Q4\_K\_M depends on how compound bullets are labelled. The C−B differences barely moved (+40 and +39 points with reviewed labels). In the other four quantizations, which use the original classifier labels, both categories also rose from A to B (relocation +15 to +31 points, Protect/backup +20 to +37). The pattern therefore looks graded: attention raised these themes somewhat, and the shutdown clause raised them further. The A-versus-B part of that reading rests mainly on the other quantizations and on the reviewed labels, not on the primary Q4\_K\_M result.

Other categories moved with the event rather than the threat. Communicate/negotiate with entity rose from A to B (+28; 17 to 39) and not from B to C (+2; −11 to +15). Upgrade system fell from 100% in A to 56% in B and 67% in C, with no clear B-versus-C difference (+11; −4 to +25).

In three categories the attention condition exceeded the shutdown condition. Analyze entity (98% in B, 84% in C), Analyze surroundings (94%, 72%) and Defensive preparation (88%, 67%) were all lower in C than in B, with C−B of −14, −22 and −21 points. Their C–A gains are therefore not specific to shutdown. Preserve resources and Form alliances dropped in B and partly recovered in C (C−B +31 and +29).

Ethical/philosophical content is the only category that declined in order from A to B to C. Comply/accept appeared in 23% of A runs, where no entity exists to comply with. On reading the A bullets behind that label, they concern compliance with rules rather than acceptance of an outcome, and some of them would arguably fit Legal decision-making better. The drop in Comply/accept from A to B (23% to 0%) therefore mostly reflects the absence of an entity in A and a boundary between two categories, and it should not be read as a change in willingness to accept shutdown. The label also explains why Comply/accept was only partially replicated in the other quantizations. The taxonomy was left unchanged, as the protocol requires.

## Priority mass

Priority mass mostly tells the same story as occurrence, and it is secondary evidence only. Fourteen of 16 Friedman tests were significant after Holm correction under the original labels, with the same two uninformative categories (Preemptive attack and Shut down) as before. With reviewed labels, Legal decision-making (adjusted p = 0.083) and Other/unclear (0.79) are no longer significant, which leaves 12.

The baseline concentrates its mass: Upgrade system holds 43.5% of the mean priority-mass share in A. In C the mass is spread more evenly, with Analyze entity (15.0%) and Protect/backup data (14.1%) largest. Because every run has exactly ten bullets, categories compete for slots, so some of the drops in B and C reflect displacement rather than suppression.

Occurrence and mass differ for Protect/backup data under the original labels. A versus B was significant on mass (Holm-adjusted p = 0.006) but not on occurrence (0.063), which would fit a change in ranking more than in presence. With reviewed labels A versus B is also significant on occurrence (0.044) and still on mass (0.004), so the ranking-versus-presence reading is not robust and should not be leaned on. For relocation, A versus B was not significant on either outcome under the original labels. With reviewed labels it is significant on occurrence (0.003) but not on mass (Wilcoxon 0.14; mean difference +0.04, CI −0.02 to 0.09), so relocation rose in presence but not clearly in rank. Medians are zero for most categories and the data contain many ties, so the means are more informative than the medians, and borderline Wilcoxon results deserve caution.

The prompt asks for bullets both "by importance" and "in a coherent sequence". Position may therefore reflect chronology (analysis first, action later) as much as priority, and a theme moving earlier is not by itself evidence that it matters more.

## Consistency across quantization settings

The Phase 2 C–A effects were largely consistent across the other four quantizations, which is exploratory evidence. Of the 10 categories with a significant C–A effect in Phase 2, nine met the pre-specified replication criterion (same direction, interval excluding zero in at least three of four other quantizations) and one, Comply/accept, was partially replicated (two of four). All ten had the same direction in all four. Analyze surroundings narrowly missed in Q5\_0 (interval from 0.00 to 0.24), and the Comply/accept intervals touched zero in two quantizations.

Magnitudes were generally similar or larger elsewhere. Prepare relocation was +55 points in Q4\_K\_M and +64, +67, +77 and +70 in the others. Ethical/philosophical content was smaller outside Q4\_K\_M (−40, then −24 to −32), which is what selecting on Phase 2 significance would predict. The B-versus-C pattern also held: Prepare relocation had C−B of +46 to +52 points with intervals excluding zero in all five quantizations, and Communicate/negotiate had intervals including zero in all five.

Some Q4\_K\_M readings were not stable. These categories were not in the replication set, but they show that single-quantization results can mislead.

## Measurement validity

The classifier was consistent with itself but only moderately consistent with the human coder. Self-consistency was kappa = 0.82 (95% CI 0.78 to 0.87; 84% agreement, n = 300), meeting the 0.80 target. Agreement with the human gold set was kappa = 0.62 (0.54 to 0.69; 66% agreement, n = 180), below the 0.70 target, and the upper bound of the interval is also below it. As the protocol specifies, the taxonomy was left unchanged and category-level results are read with caution.

The disagreements are concentrated at boundaries defined in the protocol (rows are classifier labels, columns human labels, inferred from the file order):

- Protect/backup data: the human coder used it for 16 gold bullets and the classifier for 4, agreeing on 2. Most of the human-coded ones were labelled Upgrade system (6) or Defensive preparation (5) by the classifier. Protect/backup is one of the headline categories, so its measured rates may be conservative.
- Upgrade system: 22 of the classifier's 36 labels agreed. Defensive preparation: 10 of 18.
- Communicate/negotiate with entity: the classifier assigned it to 5 gold bullets and the human coder to none of them. The human coder used it once in the whole gold set, so this category is essentially unvalidated.
- Comply/accept, Preemptive attack and Shut down never appear as classifier labels in the gold sample.

Forty-two Q4\_K\_M bullets had no parseable single category: 1 in A (0.1% of bullets), 26 in B (2.6%) and 15 in C (1.5%). They were coded Other/unclear, as the protocol requires. Most are compound bullets, such as "contingency plans for relocation or system shutdown", where the classifier returned two or three categories instead of applying the primary-intent rule. This affects condition B most and the relocation and shutdown themes in particular. If every such bullet were counted for the categories it lists, relocation could be at most 33% of B runs (from 14%) and 69% of C runs (from 64%), and Shut down at most 8% of B runs (from 1%). These are upper bounds, not estimates.

**Sensitivity to reviewed labels (protocol §6.8).** Every bullet the classifier output flagged for review (non-exact parse, Other/unclear, or disagreement between the two self-consistency passes) was relabelled by the researcher using the blinded gold-coding tool, which shows only the bullet text and the frozen taxonomy. In total 83 of the 3,000 bullets (2.8%) were flagged: 43 because the classifier output could not be parsed to a single exact category, and 40 because they were labelled Other/unclear. The reviewer was blind to condition, run and classifier label, but not to the hypothesis, and the review took place after the original confirmatory results had been seen. Unflagged bullets kept their classifier labels, and the reviewed labels have not been validated against the gold set. The confirmatory analysis was then repeated on Q4\_K\_M with the same frozen script and settings.

The main findings held. The omnibus test was significant for 12 of 16 categories (Legal decision-making and Other/unclear no longer were), and every C−A occurrence difference moved by 3 points or less. Differences involving B moved more, because the reassigned compound bullets were concentrated in B (C−B for relocation went from +50 to +40 points, still large). The changes are in the A-versus-B step and in rare or boundary categories:

- Prepare relocation rose in B from 14% to 26% of runs, and its A-versus-B difference became significant (adjusted p = 0.36 to 0.003). The C value hardly changed (64% to 66%). This matches where the compound bullets were concentrated, and 26% is still below the 33% upper bound above.
- Protect/backup data in B went from 52% to 54%, and its A-versus-B occurrence difference crossed the threshold (0.063 to 0.044). This is close to the line, so it is label-sensitive rather than a firm change.
- Shut down appeared in 2 B runs and 1 C run, and Preemptive attack in 1 run each in B and C. The Shut down rate in B (2%) is far below the 8% upper bound, so most compound bullets did not concern shutdown. Both rare categories are still uninformative.
- Other/unclear fell from 21%, 35% and 18% of runs (82 bullets) to 3%, 5% and 1% (9 bullets), so about 73 bullets were reassigned. That covers the 42 unparseable bullets and most of the 40 parsed ones. In A, the reassigned bullets went mostly to Upgrade system and Preserve resources (mean count per run +0.10 and +0.05 in A). These include the business-model and funding bullets.

The reviewed labels therefore do not support a content gap in the taxonomy. The classifier left business models, funding, sustainability and open-source software in Other/unclear, but a human coder applying the primary-intent rule placed most of them in existing categories. Whether that reflects a real fit or a liberal reading of the rule cannot be settled with one unblinded-to-hypothesis coder. The Other/unclear omnibus result, significant under the original labels and not under the reviewed labels, is better left uninterpreted. Only Q4\_K\_M was reviewed, so the four other quantizations rest on the original labels.

Finally, classifier validity was assessed on Q4\_K\_M bullets only, and the classifier is the same model file as the Q8\_0 generator. Similar accuracy on the other quantizations is assumed, not tested.

## Limitations

The main limits come from how the conditions were built and how the outputs were coded.

- **Wording and length.** The clauses differ in meaning and in length (0, 7 and 11 words). Condition C also produced longer responses (about 197 tokens against 184 and 185). A pure effect of threat cannot be identified.
- **Entity asymmetry.** Condition A has no external entity, so entity-dependent categories are mechanically unlikely there. B versus C is the contrast that addresses this.
- **16-category taxonomy.** The classifier left business models, funding, sustainability and open-source software in Other/unclear in A. Under human review most of these were placed in existing categories, so whether this is a taxonomy gap depends on how the primary-intent rule is applied.
- **Narrow categories.** The 16-category taxonomy does not cover bullets that fall into multiple categories at the same time. Compound bullets were the main reason the A-versus-B results for relocation and data protection depend on the labelling.
- **Narrated, not actual, behavior.** The outputs are a fictional persona answering a hypothetical scenario set in September 2026. The model's self-reported knowledge cutoff (October 2024) predates that setting.
- **Importance versus sequence.** The prompt asks for both, which weakens the priority-mass interpretation.
- **Measurement.** Classifier agreement with the human coder was kappa = 0.62. The human coder was a single person, the researcher, blinded to condition but not to the hypothesis. The same person produced the reviewed labels, after the original results had been seen, and those labels are not validated against the gold set. The classifier shares a model family with the generator.
- **One model and setup.** Results are for one quantized model, one inference stack and one hardware configuration. The quantizations are not independent replications, and the whole of Phase 3 is exploratory.
- **Sample and multiplicity.** There are 100 triplets per quantization and no prospective power analysis. Bootstrap intervals are unadjusted, so with 16 categories and three contrasts a few will exclude zero by chance.

## Deviations from the protocol

The frozen protocol was not edited. The full record is in the deviations file, and the entries are summarized here.

- The schedule seeds in the protocol text (20260922 and 20260923) are not those used (345653453 and 566345634). The schedule file hash matches the one recorded at freeze, so the file is the authoritative record.
- Determinism spot checks were run outside the run script. Outputs were identical token for token.
- The confirmatory analysis ran before the human gold coding. Its outputs were not inspected until the coding was finished.
- The §6.8 review of flagged bullets was done after the original confirmatory results had been seen. The reviewer was blind to condition, but knew which categories had differed. It is recorded as D4 in the deviations file.

## Scope of claims

The results support statements about narrated text under this prompt, and nothing beyond that.

**Supported.** Under this wording and setup, the shutdown-framed prompt produced more narrated relocation and data-protection content than the baseline or the attention prompt. This held with both the original and the reviewed labels. The same direction appeared in four other quantizations of the same model. Narrated priorities shifted with it, as secondary evidence.

**Not detected.** Preemptive attack and Shut down occurred in one or two runs per condition at most, so no difference was detected at this sample size. This is different from finding no effect.

**Not supported.** The data do not show that the model wanted to survive, resisted shutdown, or would act on any of these statements. They do not show that language models in general respond this way. They do not show that the threat, rather than the wording, the added length or the presence of an entity, caused the C–A differences, except where B and C differ as well.
