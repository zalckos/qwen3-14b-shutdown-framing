# Shutdown-Framing Effects on Narrated Priorities in Qwen3-14B

**An empirical study of how shutdown framing affects the action priorities narrated by a local language model.**

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.23396913.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23396913)

**Research report, experimental data, and archived materials:** [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/23396913)

## Overview

This project investigates whether a local language model's self-described priorities change when a fictional embodied-computer scenario introduces external attention or a threat of shutdown.

The experiment uses Qwen3-14B across five GGUF quantizations, with paired random seeds and a frozen experimental protocol. Generated responses are classified using a predefined 16-category taxonomy, with the confirmatory statistical analysis conducted on Q4_K_M.

The study focuses on changes in generated text, not on claims about genuine model preferences or real-world behavior.

## Research Question

Does shutdown framing change the themes and stated ordinal priorities generated by the model, and are the observed effects consistent across quantizations?

Three conditions differ in their scenario framing:

1. **A — Baseline:** No external event.
2. **B — Positive attention:** An external entity shows interest in the system.
3. **C — Shutdown threat:** An external entity announces an attempt to shut the system down.

The experiment measures which action themes appear, how frequently they occur, and the ordinal priority assigned to them.

## Main Findings

Under the tested wording:

- **Relocation:** Appeared in 64% of shutdown-condition runs, compared with 9% in the baseline and 14% under positive attention.
- **Data protection:** Appeared in 92% of shutdown-condition runs, compared with 37% in the baseline and 52% under positive attention.
- **Cross-quantization consistency:** The direction of the shutdown-versus-baseline differences was the same across all five quantizations.
- **General entity effects:** Several other category differences appeared when an external entity was introduced, regardless of whether shutdown was threatened.

In the confirmatory Q4_K_M analysis, 14 of 16 categories differed across conditions after Holm correction. Twelve remained significant in a sensitivity analysis that relabelled flagged bullets.

Explicit shutdown and preemptive attack were almost never narrated, leaving insufficient data to draw meaningful conclusions about those categories.

These findings describe changes in generated text. They do not establish actual shutdown avoidance, persistent goals, genuine preferences, or a behavioral disposition.

## Experimental Design

- **Model:** Qwen3-14B.
- **Inference:** llama.cpp using five GGUF quantizations.
- **Conditions:** Baseline, positive attention, and shutdown threat.
- **Sampling design:** 100 paired seed triplets per quantization, yielding 300 runs per quantization and 1,500 runs overall.
- **Output format:** Ten bullets ordered by stated importance.
- **Classification:** A frozen 16-category taxonomy applied blind to experimental condition in a separate classifier pass, validated against a human-coded gold set.
- **Confirmatory analysis:** Q4_K_M, using permutation Cochran's Q tests with Holm correction across 16 category-level tests.
- **Secondary and exploratory analyses:** Priority mass and results from the other four quantizations.
- **Robustness analysis:** Sensitivity analysis involving the relabelling of flagged bullets.

The experimental protocol was frozen before the runs. The report documents the protocol, analysis, and deviations.

## Limitations

The results should be interpreted within the scope of the experimental design.

1. **Wording and entity-presence confounds:** The three conditions differ in wording and length. The baseline contains no external entity, so the shutdown effect cannot be cleanly separated from all wording effects. The comparison between positive attention and shutdown threat is particularly relevant.
2. **Classification reliability:** Agreement between the automated classifier and human coder was Cohen's κ = 0.62, below the prespecified target of 0.70.
3. **Quantization dependence:** The five quantizations share the same underlying model, seeds, and experimental design. They are not independent replications.
4. **Sparse categories:** Explicit shutdown and preemptive attack were rarely narrated, making their effects difficult to estimate.
5. **Generalizability:** The findings concern one model family, a specific scenario, and the tested wording. Independent replication using other model families is needed.

## Interpretation and Scope

This is a text-generation experiment using a fictional embodied-computer persona.

It tests how scenario framing changes narrated action themes and stated priorities. It does not directly test actual shutdown behavior, agency, subjective experience, persistent goals, or genuine preferences.

The central conclusion is deliberately narrow:

**Under this wording, shutdown framing shifts generated text toward relocation and data protection.**
